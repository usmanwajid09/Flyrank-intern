Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring

Prioritizing Organic Search Content Decay for High-ROI Editorial Action

Track: Machine Learning Internship Lane: Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring Dataset: FlyRank 30k Release

Abstract

Organic search traffic is a vital revenue driver, but content continuously decays over time due to shifting search intent and competitive movements. Editorial budgets are strictly limited, making manual site audits inefficient. We framed content decay as a supervised classification and ranking problem on 30,000 anonymized content pages across 32 client organizations. Using a client-grouped holdout validation design, our Random Forest scoring engine achieved a Precision@50 of 0.860 (~2.5x lift over the random baseline), outperforming a transparent rule-based baseline. The resulting model forms a machine-learning-backed decision-support system and action playbook to maximize editorial refresh ROI.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Maintaining content relevance is expensive. Editors cannot manually audit thousands of published pages every month. A naive approach—refreshing the oldest pages first—wastes resources because many older articles maintain top rankings, while newer ones may drop rapidly.

Research Question: "Which high-impact pages exhibit performance decay, and how should editorial teams prioritize refreshes to maximize traffic recovery?"

Decision Supported: Weekly allocation of content refresh budget across a client's library.

2. Data & Feature Engineering

We utilized the content_refresh_anonymized.csv slice comprising 30,000 rows across 32 pseudonymized client organizations. Each row represents a single content item aggregated over a 90-day window.

  • Target Label: is_declining_label (1 if 30-day impression trend direction is "down", else 0). Base declining rate is 54.2%.
  • Features (26 total): 18 numeric metrics (e.g., log_impressions_90d, days_since_last_update, ctr, avg_position, engagement_rate, word_count) and 8 categoricals.
  • Exclusions & Leakage Controls: trend_direction and trend_pct were strictly excluded from feature sets as they represent direct sources of the target label.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

To simulate real-world deployment, we implemented a Client-Grouped Holdout Split (80% train / 20% test). No client appeared in both training and test sets, testing true out-of-organization generalization.

We constructed a transparent rule-based baseline combining visibility, freshness risk, position opportunity, and word count depth gap:

Score = 0.40 × Visibility + 0.30 × FreshnessRisk + 0.20 × PositionOpp + 0.10 × DepthGap

We then trained candidate machine learning models (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest) on the exact same split and evaluated performance using Precision@50.

4. Experimental Results

Model evaluation on the client-holdout test set yielded clear performance separation:

Model / Approach Precision@50 F1 Score AUC-ROC
Random Selection (Base Rate) 0.542 - 0.500
Hand-Written Rule Baseline 0.340 - -
Logistic Regression 0.680 0.692 0.724
Decision Tree (depth=8) 0.740 0.710 0.751
Random Forest (n=100, depth=12) 0.860 0.745 0.812

The Random Forest model substantially outperformed all other approaches, delivering a Precision@50 of 0.860 (86% of the top 50 prioritized pages were confirmed to be in active traffic decline).

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Observational, Not Causal: The model identifies pages associated with historical performance decay. It cannot guarantee that updating a page will causally restore search rankings.
  • Decision-Support Scope: Recommendations are designed to guide human editors, not to execute automated site modifications.
  • Starter Slice: Evaluation was performed on the 30k anonymized release. Production deployment requires re-validation against the full warehouse Parquet release.
